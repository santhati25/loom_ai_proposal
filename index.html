One unit of AI usage across every model we buy

LOOM is SQ's new LLM gateway: one controlled, auditable, cost-managed entry point to every AI model we use, instead of each team holding its own vendor keys and contracts. It tracks usage as credits, with a weekly tier for each person, and gives procurement the data to forecast, negotiate and cut AI spend.

We cannot compare vendors today

Anthropic, OpenAI and other models each price tokens differently, and prices keep changing. There is no single number for what a task costs or who is using what.

We may be paying list price

One credit is worth about $0.000015, which equals the list price of Sonnet 4.6. What we pay today is not confirmed. If it is list price, there is room to negotiate.

Prices are moving

Claude Sonnet 5.5 lists at $2 / $10 per million input and output tokens, against $3 / $15 for Sonnet 4.6 (Anthropic pricing). A credit rate card absorbs changes like this without resetting anyone's budget.

Why a gateway

LOOM is an LLM gateway: one controlled, auditable, cost-managed entry point to every AI model SQ uses. Teams call LOOM instead of holding their own vendor keys and contracts.

Without a gatewayEvery team holds its own vendor keys and contracts, with a separate connection to each vendor. With four teams and three vendors that is 12 connections and 12 sets of keys.
With LOOMEvery team calls LOOM. LOOM holds the keys and connects to each vendor once. The same four teams and three vendors need 7 connections and 3 sets of keys.

Illustration: four teams and three vendors. LOOM does not exist yet; this is the design we propose to build.

Six business reasons

Cost governance

  • Quotas per user and per tier stop spending from running away
  • One bill and one set of chargebacks for the whole company
  • Shared reserved capacity (PTU) and batch APIs at about 50% less cost, which no single team could negotiate alone

For procurement: a spend ceiling and pooled buying power.

Security and compliance

  • Vendor keys stay with LOOM, so they do not spread across teams
  • Every call is tied to a real associate or customer through required audit headers, so InfoSec can trace prompt injection and misuse
  • Traffic stays on approved, vetted providers inside SQ's network rules

For procurement: fewer keys and contracts, and a short list of approved vendors.

Accountability

  • Usage is tracked per user and per app (Druid/Kafka)
  • It feeds dashboards, tiers and ROI tracking

For finance: the demand data behind the forecast, and what each team gets for its spend.

Flexibility across vendors

  • One integration gives access to OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and models SQ hosts itself
  • Teams switch models or providers without rewriting code, and new providers reach everyone at once
  • SQ is not locked into one vendor and keeps leverage when negotiating

For procurement: a believable switch threat.

Reliability and fair sharing

  • Contention management shares limited capacity across thousands of users and apps
  • Quota and contention headers let clients handle limits gracefully

For finance: reserved capacity stretches across more users.

Faster delivery for teams

  • The API matches the providers' own, so standard SDKs work by changing the base URL
  • No per-team vendor onboarding, contracts or key management

For procurement: fewer contracts to run and review.

Weekly tiers

Each person gets one tier. A tier is a cap, not a prepayment.

TierWeekly creditsPer user, per weekPer user, per year

VP drops back to Elite every four weeks unless renewed with manager and VP approval.

Rules we propose

  • One quota per person across every surface: CLI, IDE, Desktop, Web, Slack and Teams.
  • Weekly reset on Monday at 00:00 UTC. A person's unused credits do not roll over.
  • Hard stop at the limit. New requests are blocked until the reset.
  • Upgrades through ServiceNow, approved by a manager, and by a VP for the top tier. Increases last one month.

How these numbers are calculated
  • Users by tier: total users × each tier's share, rounded to whole people so the tiers always add up to the total.
  • Ceiling: for each tier, users × weekly credits × $0.000015 × 52 weeks, added together.
  • Expected spend: ceiling × utilization.
  • Lighter-model saving: expected spend × share of volume moved × (1 − 0.33).
  • Discount saving: (expected spend − lighter-model saving) × discount.
  • Total savings: lighter-model saving + discount saving. Net savings = total savings − yearly run cost.
  • Payback: build cost ÷ (net savings ÷ 12), in months.

Vendor strategy

Five plays turn LOOM's data into leverage. The contracts stay separate; credits give us one view across all of them.

Forecast from the tiers

Price each tier and multiply by headcount to set a budget ceiling. Compare it with real use to find over-allocation.

Outcome: a budget we can defend.

Negotiate on one unit

Put the same demand in front of Anthropic and OpenAI, compare the effective price per credit, and move volume to the better offer.

Outcome: discounts, price protection and better terms.

Consolidate overlapping tools

Bring each vendor's spend under one agreement so it sees our full volume. LOOM holds the vendor keys, so teams stop holding their own contracts, and tools such as Code Puppy fold into its shared credit model.

Outcome: volume tiers and fewer duplicate tools.

Use approvals as demand data

Every request for more credits shows who needs more and why. Tiers and commitments are sized to demand we can see.

Outcome: right-sized tiers and commitments.

Score every new vendor

Judge any new AI tool on usage visibility, a published rate card, enforced caps, an approval path and contract flexibility.

Outcome: faster, fairer evaluations.

Contracts stay separate

Credits are LOOM's internal unit, and no vendor accepts them as payment. What consolidation does is let each vendor see all of our demand.

Anthropic agreement

Its own price, discount, commitment and expiry terms.

OpenAI agreement

Its own price, discount, commitment and expiry terms.

Other vendors

Each under its own agreement.

The one place a single pool is real: a shared cloud commitment. Claude in Microsoft Foundry is eligible for Azure's consumption commitment (Anthropic). Microsoft also sells OpenAI models, but whether that spend counts toward our commitment needs confirming. Anthropic's Claude Marketplace lets part of a commitment pay for partner tools, in limited preview (details).

The unused-credits push

A person's unused weekly credits cost nothing under pay-as-you-go billing. Unused commitment dollars with a vendor are real money, so that is what we negotiate.

What the standard terms say

VendorStandard terms
AnthropicUsage credits expire one calendar year after issue, with no extension and no refund. A negotiated agreement is where to change that (Credit Terms).
OpenAIPurchased credits expire after one year and OpenAI states it cannot extend them. Refunds are limited unless required by law or contract (Help Center).

Neither vendor's published terms offer rollover, so it has to be written into an enterprise agreement. Whether either will agree is unknown.

Our asks, in priority order

  • Carry-forward of unused committed spend, up to an agreed cap.
  • Extension of a quarter or more to use the balance.
  • Ramped commitment with an annual true-up.
  • Price protection so list-price cuts reach us.
  • Flexibility across models and products.
  • Credit toward renewal for any residual.

We refuse an upfront prepayment sized to the ceiling.

Building LOOM

LOOM does not exist yet. A first release needs ten components. Tech builds it, and the other teams shape and use it.

Gateway API and provider connectorsOne API that matches the providers' own, connected to Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and self-hosted models.
Authentication, audit headers and key custodyHolds the vendor keys and ties every call to a real associate or customer.
Content filtering and safetyFilters requests and responses with the same controls for every team.
Usage capture and loggingRecords tokens per request by user and app, with central logging and analytics (Druid/Kafka).
Rate cardHolds token and model weights for every model, with a change log.
Credit ledgerConverts usage to credits, totals it per user and resets it weekly.
Tiers, quotas, caps and contentionAssigns each person a tier, enforces the limit, shares limited capacity fairly and returns quota headers.
Usage view and reportsA /usage view per person, plus dashboards, ROI tracking and exports for admins and procurement.
Approval workflowA ServiceNow request with demand fields; approved changes update the tier.
Reconciliation and chargebackA monthly check of credits against invoices, and one set of chargebacks.

Three ways to build it

We suggest scoping with tech first. Tech alone can be enough if release one is metering, caps and reports. Add a product team if the scope grows. Price route C in parallel.

IT service management

For them: a defined path instead of ad hoc requests.

Risks and how we manage them

Building LOOM takes longer or costs moreScope a minimum first release, hold the gates, and use the invoice baseline so procurement is not blocked.
Tech capacity is not availableOffer three build routes, start with scoping, and tie the ask to the savings case.
Scope grows past a first releaseFix a minimum scope and add features only after the first gate.
LOOM undercounts or is wrongReconcile credits to vendor invoices monthly and publish the variance.
Teams bypass LOOMInventory spend, make LOOM the sanctioned path, and show teams their own usage.
Over-committing to a vendorCommit to expected spend, not the ceiling, with a ramp and annual true-up.
Vendors refuse rolloverFall back to extension, true-up or conversion, or stay pay-as-you-go for the uncertain share.
